# X9 execution-close feasibility correction — 1 November 2026

## Why this matters

The integrated X9 monthly-rotation backtest previously rejected BUYs that
needed same-event SELL proceeds, but it did **not** reject a fully prefunded
basket whose execution-close prices and transaction fees left the account
below the standing 5% cash floor, above 95% gross exposure, above 50%
per instrument, or with routine order sides outside the $1–$65 modeled
ticket envelope. That could overstate executable monthly rotations.

The correction evaluates the complete hypothetical basket at the **next
completed close**, using the already-fixed prior-close share quantities.
If any check fails, the entire basket is a documented HOLD with zero
synthetic orders. Funding/owned-quantity holds remain separate from
ticket/risk-envelope holds. HARD STOPs and SELL-only risk repairs retain
priority over monthly rebalancing. The original unrotated X9 simulation
remains the default and the frozen price source is unchanged.

## Limits and interpretation

- The $1 minimum is a conservative research assumption; no claim is made
  that a broker accepts every eligible fractional order or STOP dust exit.
- The $65 ceiling models the existing small pilot's routine ticket limit.
  It does **not** enlarge the $130.30 cumulative capital authority.
- The atomic-basket HOLD is conservative. A real broker could accept a
  subset, but this study does not invent partial fills or settlement.
- A historical adjusted close is not a live executable quote. This gate
  is a **feasibility diagnostic**, not a production controller or permission.
- The 2014/2020/2023 windows have already been studied repeatedly. Any
  changes in simulated CAGR or first STOP are **retrospective sensitivity**,
  not independently verified alpha.
- No changes to operator STOP, protected environment kill switch, signed
  journal, private state, capital limits, holdings or LIVE release locks.

Evidence is generated by the existing source-frozen X9 workflow and the
full LIVE static safety audit. Review their final-head results before merge.
